# Working With Me

**Houcem Hammami — Technical Manager, AI & Data Engineering**

This document describes how I operate as a manager: my communication defaults, decision-making framework, meeting philosophy, and what I expect from the people I work with. Reading it before our first 1:1 will save us both time.

---

## My Role as a Manager

My job is to make the team effective — not to be the smartest engineer in the room. I spend my time removing blockers, making architectural decisions transparent, representing the team to stakeholders, and creating the conditions for engineers to do their best work. When I'm coding, it's to stay sharp, not to be on the critical path.

I measure success by team outcomes, not my own output.

---

## Communication

**Async first.** I default to written communication — GitHub Issues, Confluence, or a shared doc — because it creates a record, forces clarity, and respects focus time. I will not message you to check if you got my message.

**Response time expectations:**
- Slack/Teams: same working day for non-urgent items
- Anything marked urgent: within 2 hours during working hours
- If I haven't responded in 24h, ping me — I don't take silence as agreement

**How to get a fast decision from me:** Write it up. One paragraph: context, options considered, your recommendation, what you need from me. I will respond to that faster than a meeting request.

**Meetings I will always make time for:** Anything that unblocks you. Anything where a decision needs to be made and only I can make it. 1:1s.

---

## 1:1s

We meet weekly for 30 minutes. This time is **yours**, not mine.

I keep a shared running doc. Before each session, add anything you want to cover. I'll add blockers I need your input on, but the agenda is led by you.

What I want to hear in 1:1s:
- What's slowing you down
- Where you're uncertain about direction
- How you're feeling about the work and the team
- What you want to grow into

What I don't want to hear in 1:1s: status updates that belong in standups.

If something is urgent, don't wait for our 1:1 — reach out directly. The 1:1 is for depth, not urgency.

## Feedback

**I give feedback directly and early.** I won't accumulate observations until a performance review — by then it's too late to be useful. If I see something worth addressing, I'll say so within a day or two, privately first.

**I distinguish between: this was the wrong call** (decision-making feedback) **and: this is not how we do things here** (process/standards feedback) **and: here's how this landed on me** (interpersonal feedback). I try to be clear which type I'm giving.

**How to give me feedback:** I want it directly, in writing if you prefer. I won't be defensive. If I am, call it out — that in itself is useful data.

**Performance conversations:** I don't believe in annual surprises. If there's a pattern worth discussing, I will raise it in a 1:1 well before it becomes formal. My expectation is that nothing in a performance review should be news to you.

## Decision-Making

I use a three-level framework:

| Level | Who decides | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| **Autonomous** | You, no check-in needed | Day-to-day technical choices within agreed design |
| **Consulted** | You decide, inform me before acting | Architecture changes, external dependencies, anything that affects other teams |
| **Escalated** | We decide together, or I decide | Scope changes, budget, headcount, cross-organisational commitments |

If you're unsure which level a decision sits at — it's probably **Consulted**. Default to a quick message rather than either asking for a meeting or acting and hoping.

**I document decisions as ADRs.** Any significant architectural or process decision gets written up: context, options, rationale, consequences. This is not bureaucracy — it's the fastest way to onboard new team members and avoid re-litigating past decisions.

---

## Technical Standards

I care about three things in code and systems:

1. **Reproducibility** — another engineer should be able to run your work from scratch with one command. If the setup requires tribal knowledge, it's not done.
2. **Observability** — I should be able to understand what the system is doing without attaching a debugger. Logs, metrics, and alerts are first-class requirements.
3. **Decision trails** — changes that affect the architecture should be traceable. A PR description is the minimum; an ADR is better for significant changes.

I don't care much about style wars — that's what linters are for. I do care about systems that are hard to reason about six months later.

## Meeting Philosophy

**Default to async.** Most status updates, decisions, and reviews can be done in writing.

**Meetings I run always have:** a written agenda shared 24h before, a decision or outcome to exit with, and notes published within 24h after.

**Meetings I will decline:** recurring syncs with no clear owner, meetings where I'm an FYI attendee with no decision to make, anything that could have been an async update.

**Standups:** 15 minutes maximum. Three questions: what did you do yesterday, what are you doing today, what's blocking you. If the answer to question 3 is anything other than "nothing", we handle it offline immediately after.

## What I Expect from Engineers

**Own your work end-to-end.** Committing code is not done — it's done when it's reviewed, merged, deployed, monitored, and the team knows how to maintain it.

**Raise blockers early.** I can't help with problems I don't know about. A blocker raised on Day 2 is a solvable problem. A blocker raised on Day 9 is a sprint failure.

**Have an opinion.** I hired you for your judgement. "Whatever you think" is not a useful contribution in technical discussions. Tell me what you think and why — I'll push back if I disagree.

**Disagree, then commit.** You can challenge any decision before it's made. Once we've agreed a direction, I expect full commitment to it — not passive resistance or "I told you so" when it's hard.

---

## Career Growth

I will ask you, in our first month together: **where do you want to be in three years?** Your answer shapes how I assign work, what I delegate to you, and what stretch opportunities I look for.

I'm particularly good at helping engineers develop in:
- Technical depth → system design and architecture thinking
- Technical breadth → cross-functional delivery and stakeholder influence
- Leadership → moving from individual contributor to lead or manager

I will not manage your career for you. I'll open doors and create conditions — but you drive.

---

## When Things Go Wrong

**Incidents:** I want to know fast. Send me a message before you have the full picture. "We have a problem with X, still investigating" is more useful than a detailed post-mortem explanation two hours later.

**Mistakes:** Own them, document the root cause, propose the fix. I have never seen someone's reputation damaged by a mistake handled well. I have seen reputations damaged by mistakes that were hidden or minimised.

**If I get something wrong:** Tell me. Directly, if you're comfortable. Via our skip-level manager if you're not. I run retrospectives on my own management decisions, and I want input.

---

## Outside Work

I protect evenings and weekends and I expect you to do the same. I don't send messages outside working hours expecting a same-day reply. If I do send something late, it's because I'm working late myself — it is never an implicit expectation that you respond.

Sustainable pace is not a soft consideration — it's a programme risk management decision.
